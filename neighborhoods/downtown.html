Welcome to Downtown Las Vegas

Downtown Las Vegas — often called DTLV — is the historic heart of the city and predates the Strip. In recent years it has been transformed from a faded gambling district into a walkable urban neighborhood that mixes historic casinos, the Arts District, Fremont East, and a growing residential base.

Major catalysts include the Fremont Street Experience, the Arts District (18b), and the Downtown Project revitalization that poured investment into East Fremont and surrounding blocks. Today the area offers an eclectic mix of bars, chef-driven restaurants, galleries, and residential options that feel distinctly different from the mega-resort corridor.

Lifestyle & community

Downtown appeals to professionals, creatives, and buyers who want an urban, walkable lifestyle. First Friday art walks, music events, and neighborhood gatherings build a sense of local identity. The climate matches the rest of the valley: very hot summers, mild winters, and abundant sunshine.

Pros include walkability, arts and culture, unique dining and nightlife, and a more authentic “old Vegas” character. Trade-offs can include higher crime in some pockets, tourist crowds on Fremont Street, noise, fewer family-oriented amenities, and public school ratings that lag many suburbs.

Housing & real estate

Inventory ranges from historic homes and apartments to high-rise condominiums and converted industrial lofts. Pricing has generally remained more approachable than many suburban luxury pockets, though revitalized blocks have seen meaningful appreciation.

  • Arts District (18b) — Creative hub with galleries, performance spaces, lofts, and urban-style developments.
  • Fremont East — Nightlife corridor with renovated mid-century stock, mixed-use, and newer residential projects.
  • The Ogden & Juhl — High-rise condominium living with amenities and city or mountain views for buyers who want a turn-key urban lifestyle.

The rental market is supported by young professionals drawn to the urban core. New residential projects continue to signal confidence in the area’s trajectory.

Schools

Downtown is served by Clark County School District. Families often look beyond immediate neighborhood schools to magnet, charter, and private options. Las Vegas Academy of the Arts (public magnet, 9–12) is nationally recognized for performing and visual arts. Other names that come up include The 9th Bridge School, John S. Park Elementary, and Explore Knowledge Academy.

Amenities & entertainment

Fremont Street Experience, the Arts District, historic casinos, Symphony Park, and a dense concentration of independent restaurants and bars define daily life. Higher education access includes the College of Southern Nevada Downtown Campus and proximity to UNLV.

At a glance

  • Population in the broader downtown area: 25,000+
  • Walkability in the core: strong relative to most of the valley
  • Median prices often below the metro average, with a wide spread from modest units to luxury lofts
  • Commute to the Strip: about 10 minutes
